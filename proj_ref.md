1. Development and Implementation of UX-Driven Playground-Style Experiential Exhibition Content (2016-2018)
https://vimeo.com/238697166?fl=pl&fe=vl - Lumi Land
https://vimeo.com/252464311?fl=pl&fe=vl - Lumi Land

해당 과제는 NRF 펀딩의 "과학문화 전시 서비스 역량강화 지원사업"의 일환으로 진행.
Design and implementation of UX-oriented playground-type exhibition contents. This project is based on five core concepts, embedded interaction, embodied interaction, collaborative interaction, open-ended artistic expression and universal design, developed three design and prototypes. There are three concept prototypes in the project: 1) Sensory Playing Seesaw : LED straps and LED display on the floor visualize the movement of seesaw in intuitive way. Audiences not only enjoy the seesaw itself, but also can understand the scientific knowledges such as  movement of force, balance, and change of energy through the visualization of light on seesaw and LED display on the floor, 2) Forest of Interaction : Sticks with springs and floor modules make audiences possible to interact with environment. It senses weight on floor and movement of sticks and behaves accordingly through light and sounds. 3) Collaborative Jungle Gym : It detects touch of human bodies between several pipes and make different sounds with the different matches of pipes. Audiences can try several connections between pipes, and it makes them interact with other audiences.

2. Usability Evaluation of Virtual Reality Fire-Fighting Training Interface (2019)
fire_fighting.png
This project is to develop a concept of a virtual reality training control room interface. 
We investigated and analyzed fire fighting simulation/training cases and trends. Also, we largely conducted future-oriented media research, collecting media scenes and analyzed them.
Based on the data, we proposed the virtual reality training control room interface. We evaluated the interface with heuristic evaluations and reflect the results to the interface.

4. Development of Multisensory Music Experience Systems and Educational Solutions for Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing Individuals (2024–2025)
deaf_music.png
This project aims to improve music accessibility for people with hearing impairments by developing technologies that enable comprehensive and immersive perception of musical elements—such as rhythm, melody, harmony, and timbre—through alternative senses including vision and touch. Based on these technologies, an educational solution supporting musical activities is also being developed.
Music education is effective for auditory training and speech-language rehabilitation, and has positive emotional impacts in daily life. However, the lack of technologies that enable musical and cultural participation for people with hearing impairments has limited improvements in quality of life. Furthermore, many existing approaches emphasize engineering performance without considering appropriate technology principles, resulting in limited social sustainability.
To address these challenges, this project will develop real-time music element recognition algorithms and visual/tactile transformation technologies, as well as a flexible platform for immersive music systems that supports various adaptations to ensure long-term sustainability. In addition, an educational music solution based on the developed technologies will be designed and validated through empirical studies in real educational settings.

5. Development of Neuroplasticity-Based Adaptive Control Interfaces for the Embodiment of Extended Artificial Bodies (2026)
abp_abstract.webp
This project aims to develop adaptive control interface technologies that provide natural control and a strong sense of embodiment when auxiliary body parts—such as a third arm or a tail—are added to the human body in the form of wearable robotics.
Unlike conventional exoskeletons that simply augment physical strength, such extended artificial bodies can enable more complex task execution through auxiliary limbs or compensate for weakened bodily functions in ways distinct from prosthetic arms or legs. To achieve this, the project will develop AI models that translate human movement signals into diverse wearable robot control signals, and design intuitive control strategies for auxiliary body parts from an integrative, multidisciplinary perspective.
In the current phase, prior to physical hardware implementation, VR simulation environments will be used to exploratorily analyze control strategies for auxiliary body parts and user adaptation processes. This foundational research will inform future designs of control interfaces that enhance embodiment and user adaptation.
